# Product Discovery

## Requirements

**Review question:** Does the team understand the user problem, policy constraints, and measurable outcome?

Requirements are learned through conversation and feedback, not merely collected. Separate the underlying need from one proposed solution. Record decisions and examples, resolve ambiguity with working slices, and keep policy configurable where it truly varies.

### Don't: build the stated solution without finding the need

A stakeholder asks for "an export-to-Excel button," and the team spends a sprint building
spreadsheet generation, only to learn the user just wanted to email a weekly summary to a manager.

### Do: separate the need from the proposed solution

The team asks what the export is for, learns the real outcome is a weekly summary for a manager,
and ships a scheduled email digest in a day — recording the decision and the example that drove it.

## Constraint solving

**Review question:** Is an “impossible” problem blocked by a real constraint or an assumed one?

List constraints explicitly, identify their source, and look for degrees of freedom outside the familiar framing. Solve the smallest core problem first. Do not evade genuine safety, legal, physical, or product constraints through cleverness.

## Collaboration

**Review question:** Are people solving the problem together with fast access to shared context?

Use pairing, ensemble work, design sessions, and short feedback loops where they reduce handoffs and hidden assumptions. Collaboration needs psychological safety and active participation; attendance at the same meeting is not enough.

## Agility

**Review question:** Can the team observe change and respond without ceremony becoming the goal?

Agility is feedback-driven adaptation, not a branded process. Deliver small increments, learn from users and operations, and revise plans. Keep practices that improve outcomes in this context and remove those maintained only by habit.
